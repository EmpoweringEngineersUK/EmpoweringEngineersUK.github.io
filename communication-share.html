What is the SHARE Model?

The SHARE model is a powerful five-part communication tool built specifically for engineering professionals. SHARE stands for Situation, Hindrance, Action, Results, and Evaluation. While traditional frameworks like the STAR method help you map out general day-to-day assignments, the SHARE model is built to focus heavily on project recovery and resilience. It provides a simple, structured workspace where you can explain exactly how you spotted an unexpected barrier during an engineering task and what steps you took to get the work back on schedule.

Why Does Project Recovery Matter for Your Engineering Career?

When you apply for professional registration as an Engineering Technician (EngTech), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), or Chartered Engineer (CEng), you must satisfy the Engineering Council benchmarks. Your assessment panel evaluates your communication and engineering leadership capabilities directly under UK-SPEC Competence D. Many early-career technicians experience unexpected delays during their Professional Review Interview (PRI) because they struggle to show how they handle sudden, real-world problems.

In a real-world engineering project, things rarely go completely to plan. A key material supplier might go out of business mid-project, a major item of plant machinery could break down unexpectedly, or site conditions might change due to severe weather. Senior reviewers from your Professional Engineering Institution (PEI) want to see how you respond to these high-pressure challenges. Using the SHARE model allows you to highlight the specific Hindrance or blocker that disrupted your work. This lets you clearly show your problem-solving skills, engineering resilience, and technical choices. It proves to senior management and assessors that you are ready to manage large engineering budgets and lead complex team networks safely.

How to Complete Your SHARE Framework Workspace Cleanly

To construct an audit-ready project recovery summary, work sequentially through the five interactive cards on our workbench editor canvas:

  • Situation [S]: Establish the primary project background, baseline design parameters, or planned installation schedules.
  • Hindrance [H]: Isolate the exact technical issue, material shortage, or structural barrier that threatened to delay your project.
  • Action [A]: Document your personal technical contributions. State exactly what calculations, design reviews, or safety steps you executed to fix the issue.
  • Results [R]: State the final, quantitative outcome of your recovery actions. Use concrete numbers like money saved, hours compressed, or safety clearances secured.
  • Evaluation [E]: Reflect honestly on the event. Explain what this crisis revealed about your team workflows and how you updated corporate design guides to prevent it from happening again.
