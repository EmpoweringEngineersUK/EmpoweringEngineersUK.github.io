Deploying the SHARE Model Framework forms an absolute cornerstone of Our Mission to democratise premium engineering mentorship, enabling developers and technical professionals to successfully overcome the structural challenges encountered within The Mentorless Maze of modern industrial asset development. The SHARE model is a practical, five-part communication tool built specifically for engineering professionals. SHARE stands for Situation, Hindrance, Action, Results, and Evaluation. While traditional frameworks like the standard STAR Methodology help you map out general day-to-day assignments, the SHARE model is engineered to focus heavily on project recovery, risk mitigation, and technical resilience. It provides a simple, structured workspace where you can explain exactly how you identified an unexpected barrier during an engineering task and what precise technical steps you commanded to get the work safely back on schedule.

Why Project Recovery Matters for Your Engineering Career

When you apply for professional registration as an Engineering Technician (EngTech), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), or Chartered Engineer (CEng), you must satisfy the benchmarks set by the Engineering Council. Your assessment panel evaluates your communication, interpersonal skills, and engineering leadership capabilities directly under UK-SPEC Competence D. Many early-career technicians experience unexpected delays during their Professional Review Interview (PRI) at their chosen Professional Engineering Institution (PEI) because they struggle to articulate how they handle sudden, real-world problems and operational bottlenecks.

In a real-world engineering environment, projects rarely run completely to plan. A critical component supplier might go out of business mid-project, a major piece of site machinery might experience catastrophic failure, or unmapped ground conditions might halt civil excavation work. Senior reviewers and assessors want to see how you respond under high-pressure scenarios. Using the SHARE model allows you to highlight the specific Hindrance or roadblock that threatened project delivery. This enables you to demonstrate your problem-solving capabilities, engineering resilience, and independent technical choices. It proves to senior management and review panels that you possess the professional maturity required to manage large engineering budgets and lead complex team networks safely.

How to Complete Your SHARE Framework Workspace Cleanly

To construct an audit-ready project recovery summary for your professional portfolio, work systematically through the five interactive cards on our workbench canvas:

  • 1. Situation [S]: Establish the primary project background, baseline design parameters, or planned installation schedules.
  • 2. Hindrance [H]: Isolate the exact technical issue, material shortage, vendor failure, or structural barrier that threatened to delay execution.
  • 3. Action [A]: Document your personal technical contributions. State exactly what calculations, design reviews, or safety steps you executed to resolve the issue.
  • 4. Results [R]: State the final, quantitative outcome of your recovery actions. Use concrete numbers like money saved, hours compressed, or safety clearances secured.
  • 5. Evaluation [E]: Reflect honestly on the event. Explain what this crisis revealed about workflow gaps and how you updated corporate design guides to prevent recurrence.
