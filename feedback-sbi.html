Constructive Performance Management: Applying the Situation-Behaviour-Impact-Intent (SBI-I) Model

Introduction to the SBI-I Feedback Framework

What is this step? The Situation-Behaviour-Impact-Intent (SBI-I) Model is an advanced, six-tier communication framework designed to manage and correct performance gaps objectively. Developed as a professional upgrade to the classic framework from the Center for Creative Leadership, this model helps you deliver clear, factual feedback. The tool divides preparation into six scannable steps: Situation, Behaviour, Impact, Inquire about Intent, Bridge the Gap, and Co-create Solutions. Using this digital canvas allows you to script your development conversations thoroughly before meeting with team members, keeping the discussion professional and focused on growth.

Why Two-Way Feedback Architecture Matters to Your Career

Why does it matter to your career? Advancing into senior engineering leadership, project management, or principal consultancy requires exceptional communication skills alongside technical expertise. The Engineering Council establishes strict benchmarks for leadership under the UK Standard for Professional Engineering Competence (UK-SPEC). Candidates seeking status as a Chartered Engineer (CEng), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), or Engineering Technician (EngTech) must prove their ability to manage people and communicate effectively. Specifically, Competence C and Competence D require you to demonstrate that you can guide teams and handle professional relationships successfully.

During your final Professional Review Interview (PRI), peer assessors look for clear evidence of how you resolve technical variances, handle drawing errors, or guide underperforming staff. Unstructured feedback often focuses on personality traits and character judgements, which can damage morale and cause defensiveness. By using the SBI-I model, you learn to separate a person's identity from workplace errors. Adding an exploration of Intent shows that you approach management with curiosity and fairness. This professional approach protects your project timelines, lowers staff turnover, and proves to directors that you are ready to manage large engineering operations.

Documenting these regular development meetings creates a solid record of evidence for your professional registration portfolio. Showing that you systematically used structured frameworks to support graduate engineers, clear up workflow bottlenecks, and manage project risks proves you possess true leadership capabilities. This structural awareness ensures you can defend your management decisions confidently before the institutional review panel.

Step-by-Step Guide to Completing the SBI-I Canvas Cleanly

How do you complete it cleanly? To fill out the interactive canvas successfully, move through each card step-by-step. Keep your descriptions clear, short, and focused on verifiable project facts, avoiding vague corporate buzzwords.

Define the Situation [S]: Establish the exact date, time, and location of the event to anchor the discussion in a factual context. For example, specify that the issue occurred during the Wednesday afternoon site clearance review for the railway asset upgrade project.

Isolate the Behaviour [B]: State only the explicit, observable actions or words that occurred. You must remove all emotional adjectives. Avoid character labels like "unreliable" or "unfocused," and instead record raw parameters, such as a team member omitting the safety checklist or missing the asset delivery deadline.

Quantify the Impact [I]: Explain how those specific actions directly affected you, the engineering team, or the wider project. Link this outcome to clear project metrics, such as a 24-hour drawing delay, an increase in project expenditure, or a failure to pass a safety gate.

Inquire about Intent [-I]: Ask the recipient what they were trying to achieve before judging the final outcome. This step encourages open communication and allows the team member to explain their perspective, uncovering potential issues with training or resources.

Bridge the Gap: Compare what the team member intended to achieve with the actual, real-world impact of their actions. This comparison helps them see how their choices affect project timelines and team safety, without creating conflict.

Co-create Solutions: Collaborate to establish clear, measurable action points and future check dates. This changes the conversation into an active Development Action Plan (DAP), which you can log in your continuous professional development (CPD) record to demonstrate long-term leadership growth.

Performance Management Workspace System

When and where did this performance event take place? Anchor your entry to a specific time, place, or meeting context.
e.g. During yesterday's 14:00 weekly design review meeting for the automated sorting conveyor layout...
What were the explicit, observable actions or omissions? Eliminate all personality judgements and emotional adjectives.
e.g. You did not provide the required FEA structural calculations, and you interrupted the presentation twice to decline minor corrections.
What were the direct, measurable project outcomes, safety risks, or cost liabilities caused by this behaviour?
e.g. This meant the team could not sign off the structural base plates, introducing a 24-hour drawing delay and risking a milestone penalty.
What open question will you ask to understand what the recipient meant to achieve before judging the final outcome?
e.g. Could you talk me through your main focus when pulling together the materials for this specific review session?
How will you outline the difference between their positive intentions and the actual, real-world project impacts?
e.g. I understand you wanted to save time by skipping the formal template checks, but that meant the cross-team layout parameters failed validation.
What explicit action points, resource supports, or follow-up review dates will you commit to together?
e.g. Let us schedule a 30-minute workspace review this Friday to double-check the validation scripts before the next project gateway.
