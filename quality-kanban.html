The architecture of a Kanban system treats process management as a dynamic pull pipeline rather than an arbitrary push schedule. By establishing an explicit three-tiered linear taxonomy—consisting of the To Do backlog queue, the In Progress active workstation, and the Done verified baseline—technical leads capture the live state of every operational parameter at a single glance. Crucially, the system enforces a rigid Work-In-Progress (WIP) Limit on the active column. By restricting the maximum number of items permitted in development simultaneously, the framework forces engineering cells to complete open tasks before introducing new liabilities. This process layout exposes bottlenecks immediately, protects personnel from fatigue overloads, and allows management professionals to calculate precise workflow cycle times to maintain complete alignment with customer delivery milestones.
