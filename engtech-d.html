Engineering Technician (EngTech) Competence D: Communication and Interpersonal Skills

Go to Interactive Workspace ↓

Earning your Engineering Technician (EngTech) status through the Engineering Council means proving you are a complete professional. Technical skills alone are not enough to succeed. Under the UK-SPEC framework, Competence D tests your communication and interpersonal skills. As an EngTech professional, you often act as the critical bridge between hands-on technical execution and high-level commercial management. You must show that you can translate complex engineering data into clear, actionable information for different audiences. Good communication keeps projects moving safely and prevents costly mistakes.

D1: Communicate in English with Others

What is this step? This covers your ability to write, speak, and present clearly in English. Why does it matter to your career? If you cannot explain a safety risk or a design change clearly, your team cannot execute it properly. How do you complete it cleanly? Give strong examples of writing technical reports, operating manuals, or project emails. Show how you change your tone depending on who is reading. For example, explain how you write differently for a senior structural engineer compared to a non-technical financial director.

D2: Present and Discuss Proposals

What is this step? This is the process of pitching your engineering ideas and defending your choices in a professional setting. Why does it matter to your career? Engineering requires constant negotiation. You must be able to convince clients and stakeholders that your design is safe and cost-effective. How do you complete it cleanly? Detail a specific time you led a design review or presented at a hazard identification (HAZOP) meeting. Explain how you actively listened to feedback, processed critical questions, and used empirical data to defend your technical proposals.

D3: Demonstrate Personal and Social Skills

What is this step? This focuses on your emotional intelligence, teamwork, and ability to handle workplace conflict. Why does it matter to your career? A brilliant engineer who cannot work in a team is a liability. You must build a positive culture. How do you complete it cleanly? Document a scenario where you resolved an argument between different engineering disciplines. Show how you promote inclusive workplace practices, support diversity, and build trust with external contractors to keep your project running smoothly.

Structuring Your Evidence via the STAR Methodology

To pass your Professional Review Interview (PRI), your written application must be perfectly structured. We strongly advise using the STAR Methodology (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for your drafts. This simple layout forces you to write about your personal actions, rather than just praising your wider team. For Competence D, your Action section must focus on the exact words, presentations, and meetings you led. Your Result section must prove how your communication solved a problem. A factual report makes your interview much easier to pass.
