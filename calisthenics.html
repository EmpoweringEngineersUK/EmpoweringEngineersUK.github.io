Training Guidelines

Why Only 3 Days A Week?

Muscles do not grow while you are working out; they grow while you rest. A 3-day split allows ~48 hours of recovery between sessions, which is the optimal window for central nervous system recovery and injury prevention.

How To Space Your Workouts

Never train on consecutive days. A standard schedule is Mon, Wed, and Fri, utilising the weekend for active recovery (walking, stretching). Consistency beats intensity.

Phase 1: The Warm-Up (5 Mins)

Do not skip this. Cold muscles and stiff joints are prone to injury. The goal is to raise your core body temperature and lubricate the joints.

  • Light Cardio (2 mins): Jog lightly in place, or perform slow jumping jacks. Just get the blood flowing.
  • Dynamic Stretching (3 mins): Perform arm circles (forward and backward), torso twists, and high knee marches. Avoid holding static stretches here; keep your body moving.
Phase 2: The Cool-Down (5 Mins)

Your nervous system is highly stimulated after a workout. You must down-regulate it to kickstart the recovery and muscle-building process.

  • Static Stretching: Hold a gentle chest stretch against a doorframe, a standing quad stretch, and a toe-touch stretch for your hamstrings. Hold each for 30 seconds.
  • Breathwork: Lie flat on your back or sit comfortably. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, and exhale slowly for 6 seconds. Repeat 5 times.

Target:
Chest (Pectorals), Shoulders (Deltoids), Triceps.
Breathing:
Inhale deeply as you lower; exhale powerfully as you push up.
Feeling:
A stretch across your chest at the bottom, and a tight squeeze in your triceps at the top.
The Step-by-Step Technique
  1. Set the base: Get on all fours, placing your hands flat on the floor slightly wider than shoulder-width.
  2. Lock your plank: Step your feet back. Your body should form a straight, unbroken line from your head to your heels.
  3. Engage your core: Squeeze your glutes and brace your abs (as if expecting a punch).
  4. Lower with control: Bend your elbows. Keep them tucked at a 45-degree angle to your torso (forming an arrow shape, not a T).
  5. Push up: Descend until your chest nearly touches the ground, then explosively push through your hands to drive back up.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Sagging hips: Puts stress on your lower back. Squeeze your glutes to stay rigid.
  • Flared elbows: Pointing elbows straight out to the sides puts stress on your rotator cuffs. Tuck them closer to your ribs.
How to Scale for Beginners
  • Wall Push-Ups: Stand an arm’s length from a wall, place your hands on it, and perform the motion.
  • Incline Push-Ups: Place your hands on an elevated, stable surface like a countertop or a sturdy chair.
  • Knee Push-Ups: Perform from a kneeling position.
Mindfulness: Focus purely on the contraction. Imagine pushing away your stress and mental fatigue as you push away the floor.
Target:
Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes.
Breathing:
Inhale as you sit back and down; exhale as you drive up.
Feeling:
Your glutes and thighs engaging. Your weight should be evenly distributed across your feet.
The Step-by-Step Technique
  1. Stance: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward.
  2. Initiate: Begin by pushing your hips back and down, exactly as if you were sitting into a chair.
  3. Depth: Lower yourself until your thighs are at least parallel to the floor, keeping your chest tall.
  4. Drive: Push firmly through your heels and squeeze your glutes to return to standing.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Heels lifting: Pushing through your toes shifts strain to the knees. Keep heels glued to the floor.
  • Knees caving inward: Actively push your knees outward so they track in line with your toes.
How to Scale for Beginners
  • Box Squats: Place a chair behind you. Squat down until you sit on it, pause, then stand back up.
  • Assisted Squats: Hold onto a doorframe or the back of a sturdy chair for balance.
  • Weight Up: Hold a heavy water jug or rucksack tightly against your chest.
Mindfulness: Ground your feet into the floor. Feel the stability and connection to your foundation. Breathe into the tension.
Target:
Back (Latissimus Dorsi, Rhomboids), Biceps.
Breathing:
Inhale on the pull (up); exhale on the controlled release (down).
Feeling:
Your shoulder blades pinching firmly together at the top of the movement.
The Step-by-Step Technique
  1. Position: Find a sturdy table edge, a low outdoor bar, or TRX bands. Lie underneath and grab the edge with an overhand grip.
  2. Body Line: Extend your legs so your body forms a straight line.
  3. Pull: Pull your chest up toward the bar/table by driving your elbows down and back.
  4. Squeeze: Retract your scapula (pinch your shoulder blades together) at the top.
  5. Lower: Slowly lower yourself back to the starting position with control.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Shrugging shoulders: Do not pull your shoulders up to your ears; keep them packed down and back.
  • Sagging hips: Keep your glutes and core engaged to prevent bending in the middle.
How to Scale for Beginners
  • Change the angle: The more vertical you stand, the easier it is. Walk your feet further back to reduce resistance.
  • Bent Knees: Plant your feet flat on the floor with your knees bent at 90 degrees to offload weight.
Mindfulness: Imagine pulling your goals toward you. Focus on releasing tension from your upper back and neck on the downward phase.
Target:
Core (Rectus & Transverse Abdominis).
Breathing:
Breathe slowly and steadily into your belly. Do not hold your breath.
Feeling:
Deep core engagement and full-body tension.
The Step-by-Step Technique
  1. Position: Rest on your forearms with your elbows directly beneath your shoulders.
  2. Body Line: Step your feet back so your body forms a straight line from head to heels.
  3. Tension: Squeeze your glutes tightly and brace your core as if expecting a punch to the stomach.
  4. Hold: Maintain perfect rigidity. Look down at your hands to keep your neck neutral.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Hips sagging: Puts dangerous pressure on the lumbar spine.
  • Piked hips: Sticking your butt in the air removes the tension from the core.
How to Scale for Beginners
  • Knee Planks: Drop your knees to the floor while maintaining the rigid core brace.
  • Incline Planks: Place your forearms on an elevated surface like a bench or sturdy chair.
Mindfulness: The plank is a mental battle. Embrace the physical discomfort, stay completely still, and observe your thoughts without reacting to them.
Target:
Quads, Glutes, Core Stability.
Breathing:
Inhale as you step and drop down; exhale as you push back up.
Feeling:
Intense single-leg muscle work and a challenge to your balance.
The Step-by-Step Technique
  1. Stance: Stand tall with feet hip-width apart.
  2. Step forward: Take a controlled step forward with one leg.
  3. Drop down: Lower your hips so your back knee drops straight down, hovering just an inch above the floor.
  4. Push back: Drive forcefully off your front foot to return to the starting position. Alternate legs.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Tightrope walking: Do not step directly in front of your back foot; maintain hip-width distance to keep your balance.
  • Leaning forward: Keep your torso upright and your chest proud.
How to Scale for Beginners
  • Reverse Lunges: Step backward instead of forward. This is often much easier on the knee joints.
  • Assisted Lunges: Keep one hand resting lightly on a wall or chair for balance.
  • Weight Up: Hold water bottles, dumbbells, or heavy bags in each hand.
Mindfulness: Focus entirely on your centre of gravity. Physical balance requires a calm, clear mind. Clear the noise and focus on a single point in front of you.
