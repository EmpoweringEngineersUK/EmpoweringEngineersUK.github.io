What is the SOAR Interview Technique?

The SOAR framework is an advanced four-part communication tool designed specifically to help technical professionals build strong behavioral interview responses. SOAR stands for Situation, Opportunity, Action, and Result. While the standard STAR layout is widely used across many fields, the SOAR technique changes the focus from simple task execution to active value creation. By replacing the rigid task phase with a forward-looking opportunity section, it ensures you highlight how you turned a standard technical issue into a real business improvement.

Why Does Structured Speaking Matter for Your Engineering Career?

When you apply for professional registration as an Engineering Technician (EngTech), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), or Chartered Engineer (CEng), you must show that you can lead teams and communicate value effectively. The Engineering Council evaluates these interpersonal skills strictly under UK-SPEC Competence D. Many brilliant technicians face unexpected setbacks during their Professional Review Interview (PRI) because they give dry, descriptive background summaries instead of focusing on their individual leadership choices.

In a senior engineering position, technical problems are never just simple duties assigned to you by a manager. Every system breakdown, plant limitation, or process delay represents a distinct opportunity to update standard design workflows, remove operational waste, or reduce long-term material safety risks. Using the SOAR technique proves to your Professional Engineering Institution (PEI) review panel that you can think like an executive business leader. It explicitly demonstrates that you understand how to link micro-level technical tasks directly to your company's long-term commercial goals and budget efficiency targets.

How to Complete Your SOAR Framework Workspace Cleanly

To construct an audit-ready, high-density behavioral interview summary, work through the four quadrants of the workbench editor canvas:

  • Situation [S]: Outline the initial technical background, system failure, asset challenge, or physical constraint your team faced on site.
  • Opportunity [O]: Explain how you leveraged this specific issue to drive continuous improvements, rewrite baseline specifications, or save company resources.
  • Action [A]: Document your individual technical contributions. Use strong verbs to detail exactly what calculations, design reviews, or safety steps you executed.
  • Result [R]: State the final, quantitative outcome of your engineering actions. Include concrete numbers like hours saved, money optimized, or safety sign-offs secured.
